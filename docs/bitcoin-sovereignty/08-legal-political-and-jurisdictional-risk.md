# Legal, Political, and Jurisdictional Risk

[← Back to the framework](README.md)

## Objective

Preserve lawful ownership, portability, privacy, and practical use across changes in tax residence, inheritance law, disclosure duties, exchange regulation, banking access, sanctions, border practice, and political stability.

This module is a control framework, not jurisdiction-specific advice. Rules change and facts such as residence, domicile, beneficial ownership, source of funds, entity use, gifts, and travel can change the answer materially.

## Risk register

| Scenario | Preventive control | Trigger | Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Incomplete tax/cost records | Independent transaction ledger and documents | Missing statements or unexplained transfer | Reconstruct early with professional help |
| Residence/domicile change | Pre-move legal/tax review | Planned move or day-count change | Pause major disposals/gifts until consequences understood |
| Exchange/bank de-risking | Clear source-of-funds records; multiple lawful routes | Information request or delayed transfer | Respond accurately; preserve correspondence; escalate formally |
| Seizure/disclosure demand | Know rights and obligations; legal contact | Official notice, search, court order | Verify authority; obtain counsel; preserve evidence; comply lawfully |
| Hostile rule/capital control | Jurisdiction and service diversification | Enacted/proposed restriction | Obtain current advice; use lawful portability plan |
| Sanctions/counterparty exposure | Counterparty and address screening where appropriate | Flag or blocked transaction | Stop; do not route around blindly; obtain specialist advice |
| Estate-plan conflict | Align technical and legal documents | Marriage, divorce, birth, death, relocation | Update both plans and test continuity |
| Border/device search | Travel minimisation and recovery planning | High-risk travel or official inspection | Follow local law and safety plan; treat exposed secrets as compromised |

## Records protocol

Maintain a protected, comprehensible record of:

- acquisition date/time, quantity, fiat value, currency, venue, fees, and transaction ID;
- withdrawals, deposits, wallet-to-wallet transfers, and evidence that internal transfers were not disposals where relevant;
- sales, purchases, gifts, donations, loans, collateral, income, mining, or business receipts;
- UTXO provenance and cost-basis method used;
- exchange statements, bank records, invoices, contracts, and correspondence;
- ownership through companies, partnerships, trusts, estates, or nominees;
- professional advice and assumptions relied upon;
- residence/day-count and major life events where material.

Keep records separate from seeds and spending authority. Encrypt and back them up appropriately; they reveal wealth even though they should not enable spending.

## Jurisdiction-change protocol

Before moving residence, spending extended time elsewhere, changing domicile intentions, forming an entity, marrying/divorcing, or making a large gift/disposal:

1. Write the relevant facts and dates.
2. Identify every potentially connected jurisdiction.
3. Obtain advice on residence, capital gains, wealth/inheritance taxes, reporting, source of funds, and estate recognition.
4. Confirm whether custody location, key holders, entity control, or beneficiaries affect treatment.
5. Review exchange and banking availability in the destination.
6. Review whether the technical inheritance plan is legally effective there.
7. Complete required actions before the change where lawful and advised—not retrospectively based on assumptions.

## Travel and border protocol

1. Decide whether any deep-reserve signer or secret needs to travel at all.
2. Carry only the minimum authority/information required for the trip.
3. Understand applicable device-search, declaration, and disclosure law before travel.
4. Do not keep seed images or wallet exports casually accessible on phones/laptops/cloud accounts.
5. Ensure loss of travel devices does not cause permanent loss or expose a signing threshold.
6. After detention, search, forced unlock, or unexplained device separation, treat exposed data according to the key/privacy incident protocols.

## Official-demand protocol

If contacted by a regulator, tax authority, court, police, bank, or exchange:

1. Preserve the notice, envelope/header, deadlines, and all related records.
2. Verify the identity and authority independently; scams imitate official demands.
3. Do not destroy, fabricate, conceal, or hurriedly move assets.
4. Obtain qualified advice in the relevant jurisdiction.
5. Respond accurately through an authenticated route and preserve the response.
6. Disclose no seed or private key unless specifically advised under valid authority and after considering security implications; ownership evidence can often be addressed without handing over unrestricted future control.
7. If any secret or device is exposed, execute the technical compromise assessment after the legal/safety situation permits.

## Banking or exchange information-request protocol

1. Confirm the request within the institution's authenticated application/site.
2. Determine exactly what period, transaction, and evidence are requested.
3. Provide coherent source-of-funds/source-of-wealth records, not an uncontrolled dump of unrelated wallet data.
4. Redact only where lawful and appropriate; explain internal transfers clearly.
5. Keep a copy of the submitted package and all correspondence.
6. Escalate through complaints, counsel, or another lawful venue if access remains blocked.

## Political-disruption continuity protocol

Prepare before crisis:

- more than one lawful communications and liquidity route;
- recovery components not all dependent on one building, city, bank, or administrator;
- an offline copy of critical instructions and contacts;
- enough conventional emergency resources that keys need not be exposed under immediate pressure;
- a clear lawful plan for dependants and successors.

During disruption, preserve personal safety and records. Do not centralise all keys merely because travel feels urgent. Avoid untested intermediaries promising anonymous conversion or border assistance.

## Validation tests

- Cost basis and beneficial ownership can be explained without relying on one exchange.
- Internal transfers can be distinguished from acquisitions and disposals.
- A current adviser/contact exists for every materially connected jurisdiction.
- Technical custody and the estate plan name compatible people, entities, and outcomes.
- Travel-device loss does not destroy or expose the reserve.
- An official demand can be authenticated without using contact details supplied only in the demand itself.

## Residual risks

Laws change, authorities differ, and lawful ownership does not guarantee frictionless access. Geographic diversification can create additional reporting, estate, or conflict-of-laws issues. Privacy measures must not be confused with concealment from valid legal obligations.
